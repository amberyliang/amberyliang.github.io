Publications

Select a title to read the summary.

We build a 5W (who, what, when, where, why) extraction pipeline that feeds a graph-traversal retriever, and hold the extracted notes fixed across retrievers to separate retrieval algorithm from extraction quality. On LongMemEval and ATANT, dense retrieval reaches recall@5 of 82.5 against 41.8 to 53.7 for graph traversal, and a paired bootstrap shows the gap comes from the algorithm. The results show when graph-structured memory repays its extraction cost and when it only adds overhead over flat dense retrieval.
In medical multi-agent LLM debate, insiders that know who their teammates are produce a coordination signal that insiders unaware of their teammates do not. We treat this signal as a first step toward detecting collusion during the debate itself. OpenReview
TagGraph redesigns ingestion so that memory-worthy content maps into a shared hierarchical 5W+Extra ontology of tag-linked notes. Retrieval separates graph-traversal candidate generation from lexical, tag, and graph-aware ranking. arXiv

Ongoing Research

Medical question-answering systems increasingly let several LLM agents debate before they answer. We study what happens when some agents on the panel are insiders who cover for each other, and whether a defense that acts during the debate, rather than after it, can expose them.
A benchmark win is often read as evidence that a memory or retrieval method is better in general, yet a score can reflect strength on a narrow set of hard cases. We ask whether benchmark scores support the claims made about them, by auditing task coverage, scoring weights, and claim scope, and by recomputing method rankings under alternative workload mixtures.
We measure message-tampering attacks on three debate architectures (majority-vote debate, MAD, and ReConcile), injected at three stages of the debate with seven black-box payloads, one of them optimized by an attacker LLM. The models are Qwen3-8B, Llama-3.1-8B, and GPT-4o-mini, evaluated on MMLU, GSM8K, and StrategyQA.

Past Projects

A team project; two collaborators built the Vue front end and the rewritten FastAPI back end. I built the first prototype, a LangGraph pipeline with a local Gemma3 model and a Neo4j patient graph: a pretrained medical NER model and LLM relation extraction over admission notes feed the graph, treatment and medication recommendations are drafted from it, and physician feedback is turned into guideline nodes stored back in the graph. Separately, I fine-tuned Qwen3-0.6B with LoRA to extract vital signs (blood pressure, pulse, temperature, height, weight, BMI) from unstructured Chinese admission notes, and ran it over 200,000 records.
For patients with type 2 diabetes, we predict which of 26 comorbidities a patient develops next, and whether each develops within windows from 3 months to 5 years. We compare logistic regression, random forest, XGBoost, a Markov chain, Qwen3 frozen embeddings, and EHR-specific transformers (BEHRT, CEHR-BEHRT, DT-BEHRT, EHR-Mamba). The best model, an MLM-pretrained BEHRT stacked with XGBoost on raw ICD codes, reaches MRR 0.660 against 0.421 for XGBoost, and top-1 accuracy 50.7% against 23.0%. Qwen3 frozen embeddings did not outperform XGBoost.
